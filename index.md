---
title: Replaying an investment thesis
---

Published by BeliefState LLC

A point-in-time replay asks what your research system could have known at a particular date. It starts with the source as it appeared then and keeps later updates out of the earlier decision.

## Save the evidence and its dates

Record the source URL, the author's identity, the security, the quoted passage, the publisher's timestamp, and the time your system first captured the evidence. Preserve the version you saw. Store the thesis, catalyst, and conditions that would invalidate the idea alongside the citation.

An example with fictional data:

| Record | Timestamp in UTC | What it establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Published note | May 4, 2026, 13:15 | The author said a factory opening could lift sales if a permit arrived before July. |
| First capture | May 6, 2026, 15:30 | The research system first had the note available. |
| Follow-up | June 12, 2026, 16:00 | The author pushed the expected opening into the following year. |

For a replay ending May 5, your system cannot use the note it first captured on May 6. For a replay ending May 7, it can use the original view and its condition. The June follow-up belongs to a later replay.

## Measure the market response separately

For a publication-based comparison, choose an entry rule before looking at the return. One reproducible rule is the first regular-session open strictly after the publication time. Use the same entry and exit sessions for the stock and benchmark. Save the price source, adjustment basis, and treatment of dividends and costs. Mark missing entry prices and unresolved horizons explicitly.

A publication-based return represents a hypothetical entry. An author's report of buying or selling is a separate record. Record a verified execution only when you have evidence of the execution itself.

## Check the result

Before relying on a replay, verify that each source was observed by the cutoff, each revision carries its own timestamp, and each measured outcome can be reproduced from the recorded prices and rules. Keep gaps visible so readers can tell where the record ends.
